# Game of Worms accessory quality standard

Last updated: 2026-08-21

This is the mandatory definition of done for every new or revised Game of Worms accessory, costume and paired prop. A unique name, valid SVG and working interaction are necessary, but they do not establish visual quality.

## 1. Concept and reference

- Accessories may be imaginative and do not all need to represent collection equipment. Regional ecology, music, food, craft, architecture and playful fantasy are valid starting points when their relationship to the location is clear and appropriately bounded.
- Do not force another field bag, satchel, sample box, card, notebook or scientific mechanism merely because it is easy to draw.
- Before drawing an unfamiliar real object, identify references for its construction, silhouette, proportions and materials. Use references to understand the object, not to copy protected artwork.
- Keep collection evidence separate from playful regional context. Never imply that a fantasy accessory was used to collect the worm or that an unidentified substrate has a known identity.

## 2. Production-size visual quality

Every object must remain recognizable without its button label at its default size in the 600 × 430 habitat.

- The outer silhouette must carry the identity. Internal marks may clarify construction but must not be the only reason the object is understandable.
- Materials must be communicated by structure: for example, chocolate needs distinct segments and wrapper or foil folds; an instrument needs a readable body, playing surface or mechanism; stained glass needs contained panes and leading; clothing needs seams, fastening and a plausible attachment to the body.
- Curves, joins, openings, thickness and overlap must describe a physically coherent object. Avoid flat blobs, arbitrary symbols and decorative lines that escape the object boundary.
- Outlines must support the silhouette without overpowering small colour areas or turning the object into a heavy sticker.
- Small details must survive final scaling. Remove illegible detail rather than relying on enlargement or explanation.
- Use a deliberate, harmonious palette with enough value and hue contrast against both the painted habitat and the worm. More colours alone do not make an object richer.

## 3. Paired-object quality

The primary and companion versions share one concept but are two designed objects, not one drawing at two scales.

- Vary silhouette, proportions, construction, internal layout and pose while preserving immediate family resemblance.
- Fit and orient each version to the local angle and curve of its worm. Worn items need a plausible attachment; held or nearby props need a deliberate visual relationship to the body.
- Keep faces, eyes and diagnostic body shapes unobscured unless the approved concept explicitly requires an overlap.
- Review all six objects together. No pair may collide, form accidental tangencies, hide another accessory or disappear into the background.

## 4. Mandatory refinement and approval process

- Treat the first technically complete drawing as a draft.
- Perform a critical review of silhouette, construction, material, colour, placement, layering and final-size legibility, then complete at least one real refinement pass before presenting an approval proof.
- If an object reads as boring, generic, ugly, ambiguous or strangely named, revise the concept or construction. A colour swap, renamed label or extra ornament is not an adequate fix for weak geometry.
- Present the exact production SVG as rendered by the real page at the default accessory size. Do not substitute an enlarged isolated drawing or a concept preview.
- Review the combined composition at approximately 360, 768, 1024 and 1440 px using uncropped full-page screenshots. Detail crops may supplement but never replace those proofs.
- Do not commit or deploy a revised visual after it changes materially from the version Martyna approved. Any later geometry, placement, opacity, colour or scaling change requires a new contextual proof.

## 5. Automatic rejection conditions

Reject a draft before approval if any of the following is true:

- the label is doing most of the recognition work;
- the primary and companion are scaled or mirrored clones;
- a generic rectangular container, badge, card or satchel has merely received a new icon;
- decorative details are not contained by the physical object;
- the object floats without a plausible relationship to the worm or leans against the body angle unintentionally;
- equivalent material parts use unrelated colours only to create variety;
- the palette merges into the habitat or the outline overwhelms the fill;
- the drawing looks acceptable only when enlarged;
- the first technically working render is being presented as final.

## 6. Interaction and technical checks

Visual approval does not replace functional validation.

- Keep both copies independently draggable, keyboard movable and individually resettable with Home.
- Verify visible keyboard focus and accessible names that match the approved object.
- Check pointer and keyboard movement after responsive resizing.
- Run the catalogue and paired-geometry audits, JavaScript syntax checks and `git diff --check`.
- Confirm no console error, broken asset, horizontal overflow or unintended change to another location.
- Update the art catalogue, location dossier and cache keys whenever a production concept or drawing changes.
