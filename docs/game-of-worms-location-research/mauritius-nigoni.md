## Collection record and evidence boundaries

The Félix Lab record identifies JU2909 as an isofemale *Caenorhabditis nigoni* line, previously M2.3, from Casela Nature Park in Cascavelle, Mauritius.

- Coordinates: -20.2914, 57.4061.
- Landscape: botanical garden/zoo.
- Substrate: rotting fruit on the ground.
- Fruit description: approximately 8 cm long, oval, with a large woody nut inside.
- Structured sampling date: 31 December 2015.
- Narrative sampling date: the end of December 2015.
- Sampled by: Henrique Teotonio, using the database spelling.
- Isolated by: Marie-Anne Félix.
- Identification method: test cross.
- Inbreeding state: isofemale.
- Founder note: the line started from an L4 female and a male.
- Same sample as: *C. briggsae* JU2910, previously M2.6.
- Elevation: unreported.

The structured life-stage field is blank; the founder-stage information occurs only in the narrative note. The test-cross partner and outcome are not given. Fruit identity, exact sampling point, associated organism, weather, moisture and humidity are unreported.

Use `late December 2015` in public prose. The exact day occurs only in the structured field. `Same sample` means a shared collected sample or batch; it does not establish that JU2909 and JU2910 came from one individual fruit, co-occurred microscopically or interacted.

## Correct local and regional setting

The collection locality is Casela Nature Park, Cascavelle, not Le Morne.

A contemporary Mauritius government environmental report describes Casela as a tropical planted park on a hill edge and places Rempart Mountain slopes in its wider setting. These features may establish regional silhouette and spatial character, but they do not identify the exact sampling point. Casela's current institutional history may establish the park identity; current rides, facilities, animal encounters, branding and present-day extent must not be projected backward into the 2015 collection scene.

Regional sources:

- [Mauritius government Casela and Rempart context](https://environment.govmu.org/Documents/eia/eiareports/2016/1605-mont%20calme/chap3.pdf)
- [Official Casela park history](https://caselaparks.com/the-park/)
- [UNESCO Le Morne Cultural Landscape](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1259/)

Le Morne is an unrelated cultural landscape associated with histories of maroon resistance. It disappears from both the scene and the wardrobe.

## Accessory contract

The three accessories are playful Mauritius references. They are not documented JU2909 collection equipment.

### Wriggle-powered ravanne drums

Semantic family: worm-worn frame drums.

- **Primary:** a broad shallow wooden frame with visible skin, perimeter fasteners, lacing and two spring-linked beaters. Two unequal harness curves fit the female's body bend.
- **Companion:** a separately constructed tilted oval frame with a different fastener rhythm, a single counterweighted beater and a shorter two-point harness fitted to the male.
- The ravanne is one of the instruments used in traditional Mauritian Sega. The UNESCO record is the source for the instrument association; the worm-powered linkage is fantasy.

### Vacoas fruit-gathering tail baskets

Semantic family: fitted woven tail scoops.

- **Primary:** a long tapered woven sleeve following the female's tail curve, with a braided berry rim, open dark scoop mouth, aqua gathering lip and one anonymous fruit.
- **Companion:** a compact boat-shaped weave with a different lattice, shorter clasp, separate scoop mouth and smaller fruit.
- Mauritius National Heritage Fund material identifies Vacoas or Pandanus leaf basket weaving as a Mauritian craft. The fitted fruit-gathering mechanism is fantasy and does not identify the original collection basket or fruit.

### Dodo-beak fruit grabbers

Semantic family: articulated carved fruit-grabbing tools.

- **Primary:** a blue-grey carved dodo head with paired crest feathers, eye, nostril, deep two-part hooked beak, violet beak tip, hinge, control cable and collar-mounted bridge. The open beak grips one woody nut.
- **Companion:** a smaller aqua-grey head with a different crest, shorter beak, separate hinge and trigger geometry, gripping a smaller nut.
- The dodo is endemic to Mauritius and appears as a supporter on the country's coat of arms. The grabber is a playful mechanism, not a reconstruction of a live bird or historic tool.

Reference sources:

- [UNESCO Traditional Mauritian Sega](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-mauritian-sega-01003)
- [Mauritius National Heritage Fund basket-weaving inventory](https://nhf.govmu.org/nhf/?mdocs-file=622)

## Scientific, cultural and privacy safeguards

- Keep the fruit anonymous.
- Do not infer elevation, weather, moisture, fruit species, test-cross partner or test-cross outcome.
- Do not call JU2909 an isotype; the public record says isofemale and leaves the isotype field blank.
- Do not depict JU2909 and JU2910 crossing, interacting or emerging from one individual fruit.
- Do not use sea, lagoon, reef, sandbar or cane as collection evidence.
- Do not reproduce Casela branding, current rides, current enclosures or animal attractions.
- Remove the unrelated Le Morne cultural landscape rather than turning it into costume decoration.
