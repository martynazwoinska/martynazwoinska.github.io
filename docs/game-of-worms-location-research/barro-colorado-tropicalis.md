## Field-experiment context

Sloat et al. report that the March 2015 Barro Colorado sampling occurred
during the dry season. At each of seven forest sites, five bait arrays occupied
the corners and centre of a 7 × 7 m field area. Each array contained six
different 6 cm agar baits arranged 3 × 2 with 30 cm spacing.

The experiment’s baits were placed on 24 March and collected on 27 March 2015.
The study tested differences in bait composition and accessibility. Some bait
types contained heat-defaunated *Gustavia superba* flower slurry: flowers and
water homogenized into a paste and then heated to remove animals already in
the mixture.

The CaeNDR QG2726 row specifically identifies its sample as an experimental
bait labelled `Cup Lutz Eq3` and describes *Gustavia superba* slurry. The
website may connect QG2726 to the bait-array experiment, but it must not invent
the undocumented meaning of `Eq3`, reconstruct an exact cup or claim that the
worm came from a naturally fallen *Gustavia* flower.

## Exact-versus-regional evidence boundary

Exact evidence:

- QG2726 strain and isotype identity
- Coordinates, forest landscape and elevation
- Collection date and experimental bait category
- `Cup Lutz Eq3` sample label and *Gustavia superba* slurry description
- L. Noble as sampler and isolator
- 18S identification and selfed line

Experiment-level evidence:

- March 2015 dry-season sampling
- Seven forest sites and five bait arrays per site
- Six baits per array in a 3 × 2 arrangement with 30 cm spacing
- Placement on 24 March and recovery on 27 March

Regional context only:

- Barro Colorado’s lowland tropical forest and research history
- The 50-hectare ForestGEO plot
- Physical monitoring from forest floor to canopy
- The Lutz Stream weir and runoff instruments
- Muted reddish soil associated broadly with Barro Colorado

The QG2726 record does not establish that the bait was inside the 50-hectare
plot, beside the Lutz weir, visible from the research station or within view of
Gatún Lake or canal traffic.

## Accessory contract

### Gustavia flower headpiece

Family ID: `qg2726-gustavia-flower-headpiece`

Scientific basis: QG2726 came from bait made by blending *Gustavia superba*
flowers with water. The flower is a wearable reference to that bait.

Primary: a broad cream-and-blush flower worn near the head, with irregular
petals, dense pink filaments, gold anthers, one dark green leaf and a visible
band and clasp. It must not cover the face.

Companion: a smaller side-facing bloom with a different petal silhouette,
stamen arrangement and attachment angle.

Both must resemble the broad-petalled, stamen-rich real flower rather than a
generic daisy.

### Golden Fleece cape

Family ID: `qg2726-golden-fleece-cape`

Biological basis: maternal toxin/zygotic antidote systems described across
*C. tropicalis* have been termed Medea-like elements. The fleece is a playful
allusion to the Medea myth, not evidence that QG2726 carries a specific Medea
element.

Primary: an irregular golden sheepskin cape draped along the back, with a
lighter wool field, contained curls, a berry clasp and a small curled-horn cue.

Companion: a shorter shoulder fleece with a different hem, curl distribution,
clasp position and drape angle. Do not put `Medea`, genotype or gene-drive text
on either drawing.

### Forest-census map fans

Family ID: `qg2726-bci-forest-census-map-fans`

Regional basis: Barro Colorado's 50-hectare permanent forest plot established
the long-running ForestGEO tree-census model. Trees are identified, tagged,
mapped and repeatedly measured.

Primary: a broad seven-leaf folding fan in emerald, aqua, orchid and antique
gold. Every panel has its own contained plot line or tree mark, while ivory
ribs converge on a berry-and-brass pivot. A coral ribbon and hanging aluminium
`50 HA` tag complete the silhouette.

Companion: a compact asymmetric four-blade side fan with a different outer
silhouette, panel construction, pivot placement, map paths and tree marks. It
has its own curved ribbon and separately hung `50 HA` tag.

The fans are a playful reference to Barro Colorado's research identity rather
than QG2726's collection apparatus. They must read first as complete folding
fans, with the map language remaining secondary. Do not reproduce real tree
identities, plot coordinates or ForestGEO data.
