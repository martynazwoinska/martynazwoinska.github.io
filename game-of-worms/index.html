The world traveller

The famous laboratory worm is also a wild explorer of short-lived, bacteria-rich places such as rotting fruit and compost.

⚥ hermaphrodite + ♂ rare male Rotting plants & compost

Tiny surpriseN2 came from mushroom compost near Bristol. C. elegans can be frozen alive in a protective solution and revived after thawing. This is the reason for its cryo-vial jetpack. An early N2 tube frozen around 1968 was later thawed to establish an ancestral laboratory stock.

For curious grown-ups: map notes and sources

Markers show selected published collection records, not complete species ranges. Dense sampling areas are represented by a few readable points. The illustrated landscapes teach the wider regional setting around each record; they do not claim that a worm was collected from every feature shown. Regional sources support each illustrated scene. “Mostly selfing” means self-fertile hermaphrodites predominate, although rare males and occasional outcrossing can occur.

  • CaeNDR strain database and Crombie et al. (2024): coordinates and ecological metadata for representative C. elegans, C. briggsae, and C. tropicalis records.
  • Félix Lab strain database: collection substrates and locations for C. inopinata, C. nigoni, and C. wallacei.
  • CGC N2 record and Sterken et al. (2015): the Bristol N2 strain's journey from mushroom compost to the canonical laboratory reference.
  • Kiontke et al. (2011): Caenorhabditis phylogeny, habitats, and tropical/temperate distributions.
  • Félix et al. (2014): species diagnoses for C. nigoni, C. tropicalis, and C. wallacei.
  • Stevens et al. (2019): comparative phylogeny and repeated evolution of self-fertilization in the Elegans group.

  • Bi et al. (2019): the C. briggsae–C. nigoni pair for studying speciation genetics.

  • Official Okinawa travel guide: the five-and-four kasuri motif of Yaeyama minsā cloth made on Ishigaki and Taketomi.

A much bigger worm family

The six worms in this game are only a small sample. A peer-reviewed 2025 study counted 86 known Caenorhabditis species. A later 2025 preprint built the broadest available family tree, placing 70 species using 2,955 genes.

Mostly selfing

Most young can come from one hermaphrodite, which makes both eggs and sperm. Rare males can still mate with hermaphrodites, so selfing is common rather than compulsory.

Outcrossing

Each worm is either female or male. A female and a male mate, and their young get DNA from both parents.

Selfing evolved three separate times

It arose independently in the lineages leading to C. elegans, C. briggsae and C. tropicalis.
